# PhotonHash v0.3 research charter

Status: **frozen research charter, revision 1**
Scope: PhotonHash v0.3 research only
Consensus status: **not specified, not selected, and not approved for Laser consensus**

## 1. Purpose

PhotonHash v0.3 is a hardware-economics research program. Its purpose is to determine whether a consensus-exact proof of work can create a credible, measurable path for a complete photonic miner to outperform the best practical electronic miner in joules per valid block attempt.

The program does not begin with a preferred hash construction. It begins with admission gates. A construction that contains optical-friendly arithmetic but fails the complete-system gates is rejected.

PhotonHash v0.2 remains a useful falsified experimental profile. Its bit-exact RTX 5090 implementation found that the matrix stage represented only about 11--12% of the optimized device pipeline. v0.3 must not rescue that profile by relabeling costs or counting operations; it must demonstrate a materially different end-to-end result.

## 2. Governing quantities

The governing economic quantity is the complete-system energy advantage:

```text
A_system = E_electronic_valid_attempt / E_photonic_valid_attempt

E_photonic_valid_attempt =
    E_optical_matrix
  + E_laser
  + E_modulation
  + E_detection
  + E_conversion
  + E_feedback
  + E_digital_assist
  + E_hashing
  + E_recheck
  + E_control_and_calibration
```

Both numerator and denominator must cover the same consensus-complete work. Setup, matrix programming, calibration, rejected candidates, error handling, and rechecks are amortized over the measured number of valid attempts. Board- or wall-power measurements must state their boundary; results with mismatched boundaries are inadmissible.

Electronic matrix share is an important rejection filter, not the final metric. For electronic photonic-accelerable share `p` and an assumed acceleration factor `a`, the familiar expression

```text
S_Amdahl = 1 / ((1 - p) + p / a)
```

is only an optimistic upper bound in which acceleration adds no conversion, optical, control, or support cost. It may reject a candidate that lacks headroom. It cannot establish photonic advantage.

## 3. Admission gates

All four gates are mandatory. Passing an earlier gate does not compensate for failing a later one.

### Gate 1: electronic headroom

- **Reject below 80%:** less than 80% of the strongest electronic implementation's attempt energy or time is credibly photonic-accelerable.
- **Serious-candidate threshold:** approximately 90% or more.
- Measurements must include optimized batching, fusion, persistent kernels, low-precision tensor paths, vector expansion, boundaries, final hashing, and candidate verification.
- The comparison target is the best implementation found after an explicit electronic-adversary effort, not a scalar oracle.

Time share is acceptable for early screening. Energy share measured at a declared system boundary is required for admission.

### Gate 2: modeled complete-system economics

- **Survival:** conservative `A_system >= 3`.
- **Credible Laser proposal:** conservative `A_system >= 5`.
- **Strong light-money result:** conservative `A_system >= 10`.
- A conservative result is the lower tail chosen before a run (normally the fifth percentile), not the median or best case.
- A candidate must also demonstrate at least 2x modeled throughput at equal board power to advance to physical validation.
- Every model reports uncertainty distributions, sensitivity, amortization assumptions, and the probability of clearing each threshold.

### Gate 3: physical grounding

- Every optical component, conversion boundary, and scaling rule is tied to primary literature, a data sheet, or a measurement with an explicit evidence class.
- Component values carry uncertainty ranges and distinguish measured results from projections.
- Admission requires a measured representative optical pipeline or a component-grounded prototype that tests the candidate's critical scaling behavior. A full production-scale miner is not required.
- The inter-round boundary, if any, must be demonstrated at the required precision, bandwidth, error rate, and calibration burden.
- Independent reproduction of the critical physical claim is required before consensus consideration.

### Gate 4: consensus suitability

- Verification is bounded and practical for ordinary nodes.
- Genesis mining remains accessible on commodity GPUs; no photonic hardware is required to participate.
- Miner and verifier memory are explicitly bounded.
- The construction has two independently written, bit-exact implementations.
- Frozen vectors cover normal, boundary, malformed, and future-dimension cases.
- The strongest surviving profile runs first on a non-consensus testnet.
- No v0.3 profile enters Laser consensus without an explicit, separately reviewed consensus decision.

## 4. Evidence maturity

Every model input and claimed result is labeled with one of these classes:

1. `placeholder`: a research-only assumption with no admission weight.
2. `literature_model`: derived from published component or architecture data.
3. `measured_component`: directly measured component behavior at a declared boundary.
4. `representative_prototype`: a measured pipeline exercising the candidate's critical path.
5. `independent_reproduction`: the critical result reproduced independently.

Scenarios containing `placeholder` inputs may compare ideas but cannot pass Gate 2. Gate 3 requires at least `representative_prototype` evidence for the critical photonic path. Consensus consideration requires `independent_reproduction` of that path.

## 5. Candidate families

The first comparison covers three families without assigning a winner in advance:

1. **Recurrent same-matrix feedback.** Reuse a programmed matrix and place an exact nonlinear boundary between rounds. Its attraction is matrix-programming amortization; its likely failure mode is repeated detection, conversion, processing, and remodulation.
2. **Tiled feed-forward optics.** Pass through multiple tiled optical stages with as few optical--digital boundaries as possible. Its attraction is avoiding a full conversion loop per stage; its likely failure modes are exact nonlinear barriers, accumulated noise, calibration, and hardware scale.
3. **Larger or block-structured transforms.** Increase useful linear work per digital seed using dimensions and layouts compatible with tiled hardware. Its attraction is specification simplicity; its likely failure mode is giving tensor processors an equally or more favorable workload.

The recurrent design is the first candidate to implement, not the leading design.

## 6. Mandatory adversarial tests

Before a candidate can advance, the research record must answer:

- Can an electronic miner batch nonce states across a fixed matrix?
- Can it fuse rounds, boundaries, requantization, or hashing?
- Can multiple transforms be collapsed algebraically or approximated profitably?
- Can partial rows, sampled columns, sketches, low-rank approximations, or predicted buckets reduce work?
- Can state be reused across nonces or rounds beyond the intended amortization?
- Does precision relaxation create a profitable false-positive/recheck strategy?
- Does realistic optical conversion, calibration, laser, memory movement, or control energy erase the modeled advantage?

A failed attack is evidence only for the tested implementation and parameter range. It is not a proof of absence.

## 7. Inter-round boundary rule

Round count is not tuned until the boundary is defined. Any boundary must be:

- consensus-exact and independently reproducible;
- nonlinear enough to prevent precomputed linear collapse;
- cheap on the proposed photonic path, including detection and state restoration;
- exposed to optimized electronic fusion and batching;
- robust to physical noise with quantified correction and recheck cost.

A candidate requiring one high-precision ADC and full remodulation loop per round is presumed noncompetitive until measured evidence shows otherwise.

## 8. Measurement protocol

- Report latency, throughput, joules per attempted nonce, and joules per valid block attempt.
- State batch size, dimension, round count, precision, clocks, board power boundary, software versions, and warm-up method.
- Separate one-time, per-parent, per-batch, per-attempt, and per-candidate costs.
- Report the best electronic result, not an average across weaker kernels.
- Preserve raw measurements, commands, source revision, machine identity, and uncertainty method.
- Do not tune the RTX 5090's firmware, clocks, voltage, fans, power limit, drivers, or system configuration as part of this program.

## 9. Decision tiers

- **Eliminated:** fails any mandatory gate or has no credible path to testing it.
- **Research survivor:** passes Gate 1 screening and has a conservative modeled path to 3x, with all assumptions plainly labeled.
- **Serious candidate:** reaches about 90% electronic headroom and a component-grounded conservative path to 5x.
- **Strong result:** demonstrates a conservative 10x modeled advantage, at least 2x throughput at equal board power, physical grounding, and all consensus-suitability prerequisites.
- **Consensus candidate:** a strong or otherwise explicitly justified result that also completes independent physical reproduction, independent software implementation, frozen vectors, and non-consensus testnet evaluation.

## 10. Change control

This revision is frozen before candidate tuning. Changes require a dedicated commit that:

1. identifies the altered gate or measurement boundary;
2. explains why the existing wording prevented a valid evaluation;
3. re-evaluates every surviving candidate under the changed rule; and
4. never lowers a threshold merely because a favored candidate failed it.

## 11. Initial primary-source ledger

These sources motivate the initial adversary and physical-cost models. They do not validate a PhotonHash v0.3 candidate by themselves.

- NVIDIA, [CUDA C++ Programming Guide 13.0.2](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/archive/13.0.2/cuda-c-programming-guide/index.html): low-precision electronic execution and batching capabilities.
- B. Baldwin, [Optical Proof of Work](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.05193): HeavyHash's optical proof-of-work construction and motivation.
- C. Ramey et al., [LightHash: optical proof of work for cryptocurrencies](https://doi.org/10.1364/OPTICA.476173), with [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.08512): experimental optical-hash architecture, photonic-advantage definition, and conversion considerations.
- Y. Sun et al., [Single-shot photonic matrix-matrix multiplication for real-time netlist analytics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-68452-x): measured and projected component-complete photonic matrix costs, including DAC and ADC terms.
- A. N. Tait, [Quantifying Power in Silicon Photonic Neural Networks](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevApplied.17.054029): system-level photonic power modeling.

The source ledger will grow with the model. Each numeric scenario must cite the exact source and identify whether its value is measured, extrapolated, or assumed.
