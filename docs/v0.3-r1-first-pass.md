# PhotonHash v0.3 R1 first-pass decision

Measurement date: 2026-07-26

Status: **continue research; Gate 1 survives conditionally, Gates 2--4 do not**

Consensus status: **none**

## Executive result

The recurrent same-matrix family is no longer rejected by electronic headroom
alone. An exact fused CUTLASS implementation on the RTX 5090 moves the
nonlinear low-bit transition into the GEMM epilogue and reaches about
93--94% conditional photonic-accelerable share at an equal-work frontier of
roughly 134 million MACs per attempt.

That is a meaningful recovery from v0.2's 12% GEMM share, but it is not a
photonic win. The complete uncertainty model currently produces a median
1.30x advantage and a fifth percentile below 1x. Its median optimistic Amdahl
bound is 15.29x. The gap between those two numbers is the unmeasured physical
boundary, recheck, control, and scaling problem.

R1 therefore passes the first screen and fails admission. Its next result must
be a physically grounded exact regeneration path, not more electronic rounds.

## Frozen evaluation rule

This decision applies the [v0.3 charter](v0.3-charter.md):

- at least 80% electronic headroom to survive;
- about 90% for serious candidacy;
- conservative complete-system advantage of at least 3x to survive and 5x
  for a credible Laser proposal;
- representative physical evidence for the critical boundary;
- bounded verification, independent implementations, frozen vectors, and a
  non-consensus testnet before any consensus decision.

The Amdahl equation is used only as a rejection bound. The complete energy
denominator governs.

## Candidate evaluated

The provisional R1-F transition is:

~~~text
state[0] = one nonce-bound low-bit expansion
product[t] = M(parent) * state[t]
state[t+1] = low_four_bits((product[t] + offset) >> shift) - 8
digest = one final binding hash
~~~

The nonlinear transition prevents ordinary multiplication of all rounds into
one precomputed matrix. The transition remains provisional: its wrap
requantization, dimension, round count, and wrapper are not consensus
parameters.

## Strong electronic adversary

Two exact CUDA implementations were built and differentially checked against
CPU integer recurrences:

1. cuBLAS INT8-to-INT32 GEMM plus a separate nonlinear boundary kernel;
2. CUTLASS tensor GEMM with the low-bit boundary fused into an INT8 output
   epilogue and ping-pong resident states.

The fused implementation is the meaningful adversary. At n=1024 and 64
rounds, fusion roughly halved the core time compared with the materialized
INT32 path. Both CUDA implementations passed Compute Sanitizer memcheck with
zero errors. A Python implementation independently matches the fused CUDA
two-round state digest:

~~~text
b32177c65a4cc26a9753316285ddb1e8
c8fca5b484789d9a4320c2a639e600a4
~~~

The current CUTLASS kernel uses an SM80-style exact INT8 tensor operator
compiled for SM120. A Blackwell-native fused kernel remains an electronic
adversary task; the present result is not claimed to be the final electronic
optimum.

The frontier is roughly flat electronically. Larger matrices buy fewer
nonlinear feedback crossings; smaller matrices buy physical size at the cost
of many crossings. This is the central architecture tradeoff, not a settled
parameter choice.

## Distinct-matrix feed-forward control

The same fused harness was run with one different resident parent-derived
matrix per stage, an F1-style electronic control. Every equal-work point used
a 128 MiB matrix bank. It measured 93.39--94.24% conditional accelerable
share and 210.26--240.62 uJ/attempt by integrated board power.

Distinct matrices therefore impose no useful electronic penalty when the bank
is resident. F1 cannot rely on matrix variety to disadvantage the GPU. Its
possible advantage is physical: multiple feed-forward stages might avoid
repeated full optical--digital conversion. Its cost is equally physical:
programming or instantiating an entire matrix bank, accumulated loss, and
stage calibration. That tradeoff still needs a component-grounded model.

## Structural probe

An independent NumPy implementation derived the known matrix checksum before
testing 64 paired states through 128 n=1024 rounds.

- 0 repeated states in 8,192 vector-round observations;
- 0 fixed vectors at every selected round;
- final symbol entropy: 3.9735 of 4 bits;
- final paired avalanche: 92.57% of symbols differed, near the 93.75%
  independent-symbol expectation;
- packed final-state zlib ratio: 0.9937;
- retaining 99% of columns produced 79.86% correct one-round buckets and
  0/64 exact whole states.

These results are encouraging but not proofs against cycles, approximation,
or adaptive multi-round attacks.

## Exactness problem

The same structural probe found values directly on quantization boundaries.
Across all tested rounds the minimum normalized decision margin was zero, and
the first-percentile margin was one part in 256 of a quantization scale.

The deliberately simple additive-noise probe produced:

| Noise standard deviation | Bucket accuracy | Exact 1,024-symbol states |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.1% of scale | 99.9756% | 50 / 64 |
| 0.5% of scale | 99.6216% | 2 / 64 |
| 1.0% of scale | 99.2142% | 0 / 64 |
| 5.0% of scale | 95.9015% | 0 / 64 |

This is not a physical noise model. It is a kill-test warning: apparently
excellent per-symbol accuracy is inadequate when every symbol must be exact,
and zero-margin results require an exact fallback or guard-band rule. The
energy and throughput of that rule belong in the complete denominator.

## Energy budgets

The measured electronic core plus the v0.2 wrapper proxy has a modeled median
energy of 254.12 uJ per attempt. It creates these complete photonic budgets:

| Target advantage | Maximum complete photonic energy |
|---:|---:|
| 3x | 84.71 uJ/attempt |
| 5x | 50.82 uJ/attempt |
| 10x | 25.41 uJ/attempt |

At 134,217,728 MACs, an aggregate 250 fJ/MAC optical engine consumes
33.55 uJ before candidate-specific feedback, hashing, control, and recheck.
That leaves only 17.27 uJ for all support work at 5x and already exceeds the
entire 10x budget.

A projected 138 fJ/MAC engine consumes 18.52 uJ. It leaves 32.30 uJ at 5x,
but only 6.89 uJ at 10x. These figures are budget arithmetic, not a claim that
either literature architecture scales to R1.

## Uncertainty-model outcome

The first component-complete screening scenario uses measured electronic
energy, a literature optical-MAC range, and explicit placeholder ranges for
feedback, digital assist, recheck, and control:

| Quantity | Fifth percentile | Median | 95th percentile |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Electronic headroom | 95.50% | 96.02% | 96.36% |
| Complete-system advantage | 0.953x | 1.301x | 1.888x |
| Optimistic Amdahl bound | 8.96x | 15.29x | 19.56x |

Gate 1 passes. Gate 2 fails numerically and is also blocked by placeholders.
Gate 3 fails because no representative R1 physical boundary exists. Gate 4
fails because the candidate is not specified, independently implemented,
vector-frozen, or testnet-tested.

## Decision

**Continue R1 only as a boundary experiment. Do not specify or freeze it.**

The exact next tasks are:

1. replace wrap requantization with candidate low-resolution boundaries whose
   physical decision margins and fallback paths can be measured;
2. build a representative detection, regeneration, routing, and remodulation
   energy budget for 2, 8, 32, and 128 boundaries;
3. implement a Blackwell-native fused electronic kernel and retain the best
   result;
4. build the physical F1 tiled feed-forward model against its now-measured
   distinct-matrix electronic baseline;
5. compare B1 block-structured transforms at the same 134-million-MAC
   electronic work point;
6. repeat cycle, approximation, relaxed-boundary, and exact-recheck attacks
   for every surviving boundary.

R1 has earned further investigation because it crossed the electronic
headroom gate. It has not earned a Laser proposal because the physical
boundary currently erases the modeled advantage.
