# PhotonHash v0.3 inter-round boundary model

Status: research taxonomy; no boundary selected

## Why the boundary comes first

A repeated matrix construction becomes useful only if adjacent rounds cannot be replaced by one precomputed linear operator. That requires a nonlinear state transition. On an electronic miner, the transition may be nearly free when fused into a persistent kernel. On a photonic miner, the same transition may force detection, conversion, digital logic, drive generation, and remodulation.

Therefore the boundary, not the round count, is the first design variable. Increasing rounds before accounting for the physical loop risks manufacturing an impressive matrix percentage and a losing complete system.

## Required boundary contract

Every proposed boundary must define:

```text
B(round, state, public_parameters) -> next_state
```

and specify all of the following:

- exact input and output alphabets;
- integer or bit-level reference behavior, including ties and saturation;
- where nonlinearity occurs and why it prevents linear collapse;
- electronic fusion and batching strategy;
- optical sensing, decision, routing, storage, and remodulation path;
- precision, noise margin, calibration cadence, and correction path;
- energy and latency per boundary at a declared system boundary;
- probability and cost of recheck or fallback;
- an attack plan for approximation, state reuse, and partial evaluation.

An optical diagram without a bit-exact transition is not a candidate. A bit-exact transition without a physical costed path is not a candidate either.

## Boundary families

### 1. Full ADC, digital nonlinearity, and remodulation

**Shape.** Detect every output, digitize it, apply requantization or another nonlinear function digitally, then drive the next optical input.

**Cryptographic strength.** Straightforward to specify and test. Saturating quantizers, bucket maps, and keyed permutations can make linear collapse impossible.

**Electronic adversary.** Highly fuseable. Tensor output can feed integer clamp, shuffle, and the next matrix operation without leaving on-chip memory.

**Physical burden.** Pays detection, ADC, state movement, nonlinear logic, DAC or driver, and modulation on every round. This is the recurrent design's default kill mechanism.

**Advance condition.** A representative loop must demonstrate exact decisions at the target rate and a conservative total boundary energy low enough for the whole candidate to clear 5x.

### 2. Low-resolution threshold or sign regeneration

**Shape.** Use comparators, sense amplifiers, or low-resolution conversion to map each accumulated output to a sign, bit, ternary symbol, or a small number of buckets.

**Cryptographic strength.** Nonlinear and exact when margins are unambiguous. Low state entropy or symmetric thresholds may create cycles, correlations, or approximation attacks.

**Electronic adversary.** Very cheap to fuse. Bit packing and binary or low-bit tensor paths may outperform the nominal electronic baseline, so the strongest representation must be benchmarked.

**Physical burden.** Potentially avoids high-resolution ADCs, but still needs reliable threshold references, detection, regeneration, routing, and next-stage drive. Near-threshold results create correction or recheck cost.

**Advance condition.** Show adequate state entropy, cycle behavior, minimum decision margins, measured low-resolution regeneration energy, and no profitable uncertain-value strategy.

### 3. Threshold plus fixed permutation, sign, or bias

**Shape.** Apply a low-resolution nonlinear decision, then a public round-dependent permutation, sign flip, sparse mixing, or bias before the next transform.

**Cryptographic strength.** Round variation can frustrate fixed points and symmetries, but linear decorations alone add no collapse resistance beyond the threshold.

**Electronic adversary.** Permutations may be free through addressing or expensive if they break tensor layouts. Both fused and materialized forms must be measured.

**Physical burden.** Passive routing and sign representation may be cheap in a feed-forward layout; reconfigurable routing and active bias can add loss, control energy, or calibration complexity.

**Advance condition.** The exact round variation must have a concrete passive or amortized physical realization and survive an electronic layout optimization.

### 4. Feed-forward nonlinear stages with one final conversion

**Shape.** Cascade tiled optical linear stages and implement nonlinear regeneration within the optical or mixed-signal path, postponing full digital conversion until the end.

**Cryptographic strength.** Potentially supplies many non-collapsible stages. The consensus transition must still be reproducible despite analog accumulation and device variation.

**Electronic adversary.** A GPU can keep the complete state resident and fuse each low-cost nonlinearity between GEMMs. The comparison must include this persistent implementation.

**Physical burden.** Avoids repeated full conversion, but incurs optical loss, fan-out or routing, nonlinear-device energy, stage-to-stage noise, calibration, and a larger fabricated pipeline.

**Advance condition.** Demonstrate or ground a stage whose nonlinear behavior has deterministic margins, then show that noise and energy scale across the intended depth without round-by-round digital correction.

### 5. Stateful optical recurrence

**Shape.** Retain or recirculate an optical or mixed-signal state while reusing one programmed transform, with a physical nonlinear element closing the loop.

**Cryptographic strength.** Matrix programming is maximally amortized. The recurrence must avoid short cycles and predictable basins across public matrices and nonce seeds.

**Electronic adversary.** Persistent kernels and batched state matrices are the natural baseline. Long recurrences may be memory-light and exceptionally GPU-friendly.

**Physical burden.** Loop loss, phase or amplitude drift, storage lifetime, synchronization, nonlinear regeneration, and periodic calibration may dominate. Exactness cannot rely on indefinitely preserving an analog value.

**Advance condition.** A representative loop must demonstrate stable exact symbolic regeneration for the required rounds and throughput, including its control and calibration energy.

## First experimental boundary: exact low-bit wrap requantization

The first software candidate will use an explicitly provisional boundary because it is easy to make bit-exact and exposes the economic problem cleanly:

```text
sum_i = sum_j M[i,j] * state[j]
bucket_i = ((sum_i + offset) >> shift) & (2^bits - 1)
next_state[i] = bucket_i - 2^(bits - 1)
```

The exact scale, rounding rule, alphabet, and round-mask derivation are deliberately unfrozen. Before specification work, experiments must establish:

- output distribution and entropy across parents, nonces, and rounds;
- cycle and convergence behavior;
- decision-margin distribution under realistic noise;
- GPU fusion cost for 1, 2, 4, and more bits;
- comparator, low-resolution ADC, routing, and drive requirements;
- whether round variation is necessary to avoid symmetries, and what physical
  control cost it would impose.

This boundary is an instrumented probe, not a proposed consensus primitive.

## Kill criteria

A boundary is eliminated when any of the following holds with no credible measured route around it:

- full loop cost makes the fifth-percentile complete-system advantage less than 3x;
- exactness requires high-resolution conversion on every round;
- near-boundary correction or recheck erases the advantage;
- an electronic representation makes the required linear work no longer dominant;
- the state collapses into short cycles, low entropy, or an exploitable approximation;
- calibration or drift correction scales at least as badly as the saved electronic work;
- the physical implementation assumes an unavailable device without an experimentally defensible scaling path.

## Required experiment record

Each boundary experiment records a machine-readable manifest with:

- source revision and candidate identifier;
- dimension, rounds, alphabet, batch size, and matrix lifetime;
- electronic kernel and fusion strategy;
- physical architecture and evidence references;
- per-component energy distributions;
- exactness and error/recheck measurements;
- attack results;
- charter-gate outcome and reason.

No successful timing result changes the consensus status. Advancement is a research decision under the charter, not an implicit protocol freeze.
